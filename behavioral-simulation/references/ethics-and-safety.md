# Ethics, privacy, and safety

Behavioral simulation can create a false sense that a computational proxy *is* a person or population. The method must resist that framing.

## Identity and disclosure

- Clearly disclose synthetic/agent status.
- Do not deceptively impersonate a real person.
- Do not imply consciousness, agency, or consent by the simulated agent.

## Person-grounded data

Rich interviews and traces can enable the model to infer attributes not directly stated.

- minimize data;
- restrict access;
- aggregate by default;
- define retention;
- separate consent for research from reuse when appropriate;
- avoid exposing individual free-form agent responses when aggregate results suffice.

## High-stakes decisions

Do not use behavioral simulation as the sole basis for consequential decisions about a real person in medicine, credit, employment, insurance, legal status, safety, or similarly high-stakes domains.

## Stakeholder substitution

Simulation is especially useful before fieldwork or where risky hypotheses should be screened cheaply. It should not displace people whose lived outcomes are materially affected by the decision.

## Audit

Log:

- model/version;
- material prompts/config;
- data provenance;
- run date;
- outputs used for decisions;
- validation/calibration basis;
- human decision owner.

## Persuasion and manipulation

Person-grounded models increase the risk of tailored persuasion. Do not use this method to optimize manipulative targeting or exploit sensitive inferred traits.
